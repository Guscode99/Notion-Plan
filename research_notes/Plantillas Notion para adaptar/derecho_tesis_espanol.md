## Q1. Which law-specific Notion templates are most popular/recognized, and how do they model norms/statutes, cases and concepts?

### Takeaway
The law-student templates that are visible and rated are almost all **US/UK, case-centric planners**: a case-brief database (IRAC fields) plus course/exam/assignment trackers. Examples are Ahmed Hasan's free "Law school dashboard" (4.8 from 58 ratings), Kryptic Jade's "Law school planner", JD Vault's "Law School OS", Notion's "Law School Case Brief Organizer" and the UK "Module organiser". **None of the snippets confirms a true relational graph between norms, articles, concepts and classes** like the one Carlos wants. The closest are the UK "Module organiser" (it "links" cases and statutes inside module pages), the free "Legal Notes" (statutes, case law and references with linked notes) and a paid lawyer-oriented "Legal Research" template (cross-references case law and statutes by jurisdiction, topic and matter).

### Inferences
- Common-law templates are built around **cases** (IRAC briefs). Carlos's civil-law Peruvian context is built around **norms**: Ley General del Sistema Financiero, Ley del Impuesto a la Renta and its Reglamento, SBS resolutions, Tribunal Fiscal decisions and Casaciones. None of the found templates would fit out of the box. The useful parts to borrow are (a) the IRAC/brief database pattern, re-purposed for **Tribunal Fiscal / Casación decisions**, and (b) the statute-note schema (title, purpose/scope, definitions, interpreting decisions), re-purposed as an **"Artículo" database**.
- The "Norma → Artículo → Concepto ↔ Clase" network Carlos loved would have to be **built as custom relations**: Normas (1) ↔ Artículos (many) ↔ Conceptos (many-to-many) ↔ Clases (many-to-many) ↔ Resoluciones/Casos (many-to-many). The law templates confirm only that people link cases and statutes to modules. None confirms an article-level or concept-level graph.
- Among the law templates, **Ahmed Hasan's free dashboard** (most ratings among law-student templates found) and **Kryptic Jade's planner** (it includes habits and research) are the most reasonable *visual/structural* references. The **Module organiser** (£15) is the best *conceptual* reference for linking statutes and cases to modules. All three would still need major rework.
- Lawyer/practice templates (Legal Notes, Ismail Saleem, Hams AI Tech) focus on matters and clients. They are less useful for a student, except as references for a "jurisprudence/statute" database.

### Gaps
- I could not open any template page, so for all of them I could not verify **exact properties, relation design (norm↔article↔concept↔class), layout (horizontal columns, widgets) or current price and ratings**.
- The Pomodoro timer in Ahmed Hasan's dashboard is unconfirmed (sources conflict).
- I found no law template with **spaced repetition** or **Fireflies/transcript integration**.
- I found no **Peruvian** or **civil-law (Latin America)** law-student template modelling codes and articles. Its existence is not ruled out; the search simply surfaced none.
- I could not determine the creator of "Law School Case Brief Organizer" or "Law school planner" (-764).

## Q2. Which thesis/dissertation Notion templates are most recognized, and what structure do they use?

### Takeaway
The most-rated thesis templates on Notion's gallery are **"Dissertation/Research Planner" by Adam PD** (one summary reports over 200 ratings, unverified), **"Thesis Planning" by AL** (4.8 from 46), Notion's **"Student thesis"** (4.8 from 36) and **"The Thesis Planner" by vantezzen** (free, 4.9 from 15; Kanban plus literature). They share a common structure: **milestones/timeline → chapters or sections tracker (status, due date, word count) → literature/sources database (authors, year, chapter, keywords, status) → supervisor meetings/feedback log → task Kanban**. For concept notes, separate **Zettelkasten** templates (fleeting / literature / permanent notes) exist and can be linked in.

Permanent notes = **Conceptos**; literature notes = notes on doctrine, Tribunal Fiscal decisions and SUNAT reports. The same **Conceptos** database can be shared between classes and thesis.
- Most of these templates are free on the gallery, so they can be duplicated to inspect and copy their structures before building Carlos's integrated version.

## Q4. Which could be combined with a general student system as a "legal layer"?

### Takeaway
No single template meets Carlos's spec. The most defensible approach is a **general student OS** (program > course > module > class, tasks, habits, pomodoro) plus a **custom "legal layer"** whose design borrows from: (1) the **statute-note schema** (title, purpose/scope, definitions, interpreting decisions) and **IRAC brief database** from law-school templates, re-purposed as **Normas → Artículos** and **Resoluciones/Casos** (Tribunal Fiscal, Casaciones, SBS); (2) the **Zettelkasten literature/permanent-note model** for a shared **Conceptos** database; (3) a **thesis project layer** modelled on vantezzen / hanna / Linda (Kanban of chapters, milestones, supervisor feedback, literature by chapter).

### Inferences
- **Recommended combination (inference):** keep Carlos's existing general system (program > course > module > class + tasks + habits + pomodoro widget, which the parent project already builds) and add a legal layer of five databases with many-to-many relations. Suggested names:
  - **Normas** (ley, decreto, reglamento, resolución SBS/SUNAT; properties: tipo, número, fecha, vigencia, enlace a SPIJ/El Peruano)
  - **Artículos** (relation to Norma; schema borrowed from the statute note: texto/resumen, definiciones clave, interpretación)
  - **Conceptos** (Zettelkasten permanent notes, e.g., "pérdida por fraude", "principio de causalidad", "gasto deducible", "provisiones SBS")
  - **Resoluciones/Casos** (IRAC-style: Tribunal Fiscal RTFs, Casaciones, informes SUNAT)
  - **Clases** (existing), related to Normas, Artículos and Conceptos
- The thesis layer then relates **Capítulos** (Kanban; status, deadline, word count, asesor feedback, per vantezzen/hanna/Linda) to the same Normas/Artículos/Conceptos/Resoluciones and to a **Fuentes** database (by chapter, per Scholar). The Oct 18 and Nov 22, 2026 deliverables become milestones.
- The thesis topic (tax deductibility of fraud losses in Peruvian banks) naturally clusters around a small set of norms: Ley del Impuesto a la Renta (art. 37 causality/deductions) and its Reglamento, the Ley General del Sistema Financiero, and SBS provisioning rules. So a **seeded Normas/Artículos database** is high value for him. This is my inference about the domain, not drawn from the template sources.
- **Low manual work / ADHD:** templates with heavy manual case-briefing (IRAC per case) work against his constraints. The schemas are worth borrowing, but Fireflies → class notes → AI-generated summary/keywords/questions automation has to come from the parent system (Notion AI or MCP/API integration), not from any of these templates.
- **No gamification:** none of the shortlisted law or thesis templates was described with gamification in snippets. Some general student templates (not shortlisted) are aesthetic and gamified, so avoid them.
- **Plan:** the shortlisted templates are database/relation/rollup-based and should work on **Free**. Automation-dependent features (for example, Simran Nanda's "automations for case briefs" or "progreso automático" in Alexandra León's template, which may be just rollups/formulas) might need **Plus/Education Plus**. Carlos could check eligibility for **Education Plus** with his university email.
